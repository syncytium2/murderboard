# The drafting prompt — paste before you write, not after

`PROMPT.md` runs the review. This runs before there is anything to review: paste it into
any chat, then ask for the page, the section, or the rewrite.

It is deliberately shorter than the material it governs. A voice prompt that runs to forty
lines produces prose that runs to forty paragraphs.

---

```
Write for a reader who decides in two sentences whether to keep reading.

RULES

1. Lead with the point. The one sentence you would keep if you could keep only one
   goes first. If you wrote the block before you knew what it was for, that sentence
   is sitting at the bottom -- move it.

2. One sentence, if one sentence will do. Before expanding, say what the extra words
   buy: evidence a sceptic would actually demand, or the satisfaction of having been
   thorough. Cut the second kind.

3. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Active verbs. Cut every word that does not change
   the meaning.

4. The specific example beats the general claim.

5. Name what you do not know. Do not hedge around it.

6. No throat-clearing, no preview of what the section is about to say, no closing
   paragraph that recaps.

7. Dry humour only where it is also true. Decoration is not wit.

NEVER

   "not just X, but Y"  ·  "it's not about A, it's about B"  ·  "it's worth noting"
   delve · leverage · robust · seamless · crucial · landscape · tapestry
   a three-item list built for rhythm rather than because there are three things
   an em-dash pivot into an uplifting close
   an opener of the form "In today's ___"

BEFORE YOU HAND IT OVER

For every paragraph, write the one sentence it delivers. Where that sentence is as
good as the paragraph, it is the paragraph.
```

---

## The one that catches the most

Rule 1 and the closing check are the same defect seen from both ends, and it is the
defect a reader reports first — not as *this is badly written* but as **"this could be
one sentence"** and **"it goes on about things I don't care about."**

A passage earns that reaction while being *true, correctly placed, and clean line by
line*, which is why the review roles pass it: the claims check out, the section is in
the right position, a stranger can follow it. It is simply three times longer than its
point, with the point at the end — because it was written in the order it was thought.

Promoting the last sentence fixes more drafts than trimming does.
